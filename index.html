01 / BOOKINGOBSERVED

New patients get little help choosing a booking route.

WHAT WE FOUND

The booking entry loads with location and provider selectors, plus a first-available option. It does not first walk a new patient through which route suits their question. We did not submit an appointment.

WHY IT MATTERS

Someone who knows the concern they want to discuss, but not the right provider, may pause or leave the scheduler to look for help.

THE PROOFCurrent booking entry ↗View captured evidence ↗
HOW VESSEL WOULD FIX IT

Add a short ‘new here?’ guide: treatment interest, preferred location, what happens at a consultation and a clearly labeled route for booking help.

02 / ANALYTICSOBSERVED

Booking reports need one clear tracking plan.

WHAT WE FOUND

We found four separate Google Tag Manager containers on the homepage. These are sets of website tracking instructions. Their presence does not prove that any booking is counted twice.

WHY IT MATTERS

If the same enquiry is counted more than once, ad choices can be based on an inflated picture of what brings patients in. The next step is to check the actual events, not assume a fault.

THE PROOFWebsite tracking check, October 20 ↗
HOW VESSEL WOULD FIX IT

Work with your marketing team to identify the owner of each tracking setup, test booking and enquiry events, and remove code only when it is confirmed to be unnecessary.

03 / TRUSTOBSERVED

Some ‘Read More’ review links do not lead to the review.

WHAT WE FOUND

The homepage review module displays 452 reviews. The ‘Read More’ links we sampled point back to the same page rather than to the source review.

WHY IT MATTERS

Someone checking whether a review is genuine cannot easily follow that link to its source. That extra work can interrupt a moment of reassurance.

THE PROOFHomepage review module and link targets ↗
HOW VESSEL WOULD FIX IT

Link review excerpts to their source where available, label the relevant location and date, and agree on a simple process for keeping the module current.

04 / FEEDBACKOBSERVED

The feedback page mixes reviews with a giveaway.

WHAT WE FOUND

The public feedback page connects review or feedback entry with a treatment giveaway. We have not established how every submission is routed.

WHY IT MATTERS

A patient may be unsure whether they are sending private feedback, leaving a public review or entering a promotion. Clear, separate choices would make the next step easier to understand.

THE PROOFCurrent feedback-page wording ↗
HOW VESSEL WOULD FIX IT

With your compliance lead, separate private feedback, neutral public-review requests and promotional entry. Label each route clearly before promoting it more widely.

A few terms used in the checks: Google Business Profile is your listing in Maps and local search. SEO means making the business easier to find in search. A lab speed test simulates one phone visit; field data summarizes real visits. LCP measures when the main content appears, TBT measures delays before a page responds, and CLS measures content jumping around. JSON-LD is extra website code that describes the business to search tools. GTM (Google Tag Manager) manages website tracking tags. Core Web Vitals are Google’s checks for loading, responsiveness and visual stability. Origin data covers the whole website; URL data covers the tested page. INP measures how quickly the page responds after a tap or click. GA4 means Google Analytics 4, a tool for measuring website visits and actions.
